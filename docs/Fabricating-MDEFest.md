# Reflections #

The two week sprint has been both very important and fun for me; I finally had the chance to fully work on my project and only on my project, which has been something not very frequent in the previous months. I could finally fully concentrate for multiple days on the outcome that I wanted and since I'm not a multitasking type of person this really helped me a lot. As I started to organize the calendar in the first day of the first intensive week and after I talked with the professionals, I quickly realised that one part of what I wanted to do for the final exhibition had to be cancelled. I wanted to design a machine to shred the textile that I would then have recycled into new objects, but the actual steps and trials that would have take me to get to develop this was way too long for the few time I had. I decided to cut this part of the project off, and so another need arised, I needed to find a big quantity of textile scraps already shredded in order to quickly start creating my final artifact to showcase my research. Finding the material took me a long time and I actually had to pay for it because it was shredded and treated as it was used for application of insulation in houses; this was a pretty bad news, but the good one is that I found another place that would give me bigger size scraps for free, which were not convenient for me at the moment, but will be soon, as I am wishing to continue on with my research and willing to develop the machine with more calm. I definitely have to say anyway that the sprint week made me crush quickly with the reality and the complexity of what I wanted to do and going through this early in the process was very helpful as it made me rearrange the plan in a realistic way. In the second part of the sprint, I finally managed to get the hand on the material and thus started to do lots of testing and experimenting. I tried cutting, drilling and screwing the material, I did different composition tests and also I finally decided what my final artifact would have been: an armchair made fully of textile. This goal was ambitious and some sort of a bet, as I never did something this big with the material before; many struggles and problems came up, but in the end I managed to have a satisfactory looking piece that properly showcased the features of the material. Making the mold was a long process, mixing 13 kg of textile with double amount of glue was a super time consuming process, I even had to ask for the help of some friends, but in the end the artifact came together in time. This two sprint session have been quite intense, with a lot of work and struggle in some points, but I've been really happy about what they allowed me to do. Both the professional guest and FabLab stuff gave me a big helping hand in directing my effort and understanding the nuances of the process and the concept. Also, as much as it was a bit of a struggle to fill it during the weeks, the miro board in the end really helped me visualize the development of my project and understand better the changes of direction and the right modifications I had to do. Overall I felt this weeks were super productive for me and I enjoyed diving deep into the fabrication as a nice temporary ending of my research.
